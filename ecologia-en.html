01. Global Warming

A sea growing warmer

The Mediterranean Sea is warming faster than the global ocean average: it is a climate change "hot spot", a place where the effects are felt earlier and more strongly than elsewhere.

Warming is not just a matter of a few extra degrees: it changes currents, prey availability, reproductive periods and, with them, the timing and routes of the migrations that the tuna fishery economy depended on.

+1.5 °C Mediterranean warming vs. pre-industrial levels
~20% Faster than the global ocean average
>26 °C Above this temperature bluefin tuna reproduction becomes critical

Why it matters to us

The Gallipoli tuna fishery existed for centuries because bluefin tuna passage was predictable: every spring the shoals crossed the Mediterranean along the same path. That predictability is the invisible link between the museum and the climate.

Climate is rewriting the historical route
Migration & Climate

Tuna routes are changing

Bluefin tuna is not disappearing: it is changing home. And with it, the balance of the entire Mediterranean is shifting.

An international study led by AZTI analysed how rising ocean temperatures are changing, and will continue to change, the feeding and spawning areas of bluefin tuna (Thunnus thynnus).

Projections indicate that the Mediterranean will progressively lose quality as a spawning habitat, while waters at higher latitudes — Northern Europe, the north-eastern Atlantic, Greenland — become increasingly favourable. True "climate refugia" are forming where tuna and their prey find optimal conditions.

The risk is not only biological but also managerial: a species on the move is harder to govern with tools designed for a sea that is no longer what it used to be.

From the museum's memory

For centuries the tonnara intercepted tuna in spring along the Ionian coast of Salento. Today the museum tells not only a past of nets and mattanza: it also tells the question that climate change poses about the future of the sea. See the migratory route and the timeline.

02. The Threats

A sea to protect

Thermal stress is compounded by pollution. Every year millions of tonnes of plastic end up in the oceans: waste that fragments into microplastics, enters the food chain and reaches our tables.

Then there are ghost nets — lost fishing gear that keeps "fishing" for years without control — and the contaminants that build up in top predators such as tuna. Protecting the sea means protecting the communities that have lived from it for centuries.

8 M t/yr Plastic entering the oceans every year
2050 If we do not act, more plastic than fish (by weight)
SDG 14 UN goal "Life Below Water" (Agenda 2030)

What we can do

Prevent Reduce single-use plastic and rethink packaging: the best solution is the one that produces no waste.
Clean up Recover waste and ghost nets, with organised cleanups and monitoring of collected data.
Study Protection also passes through marine protected areas such as the Pelagos Sanctuary: see ECOLOGIA DEI GRANDI PELAGICI DEL MAR LIGURE.pdf.
Scholarship · Sea Prize

Ideas for a Clean Sea

An annual scholarship open to everyone with a sensible idea for cleaning the sea from pollution. The best ideas are presented and awarded at the museum, which becomes the meeting point and starting point of initiatives for the defence of the sea.

01

Young People and Schools

Students aged 14 to 19, also as groups or classes: a concrete and feasible idea, presented as a short submission. The winning project receives a scholarship and is hosted in an exhibition at the museum.

Criteria: feasibility, impact, originality, openness of content (free licence).
02

Researchers, Start-ups and Citizens

Anyone with a concrete solution to prevent or remove pollution: technologies, services, citizen science actions. Winners receive a contribution to develop the idea, a mentoring path and the chance to present it at a public day at the museum.

Criteria: effectiveness, sustainability, reproducibility, benefit for the local community.

How it works: applications open in spring, the jury (museum scientific committee, scholars and partners) reviews submissions in summer, and the award ceremony takes place during the Day of the Sea in autumn, at the museum.

Openness: submitted ideas, with the authors' consent, will be published in an open, machine-readable format (CC0), consistent with the project's data catalogue philosophy.

Demonstrative initiative: the call is presented as a project under development and the form below is demonstrative (no submission to a server).

A Meeting and Starting Point

The museum opens to the future of the sea

The museum is not only the guardian of the past: it is the place where the community, schools and researchers meet to think about and act for the sea.

The Day of the Sea

An annual event at the museum: the Scholarship award ceremony, meetings with researchers and associations, stories from those who live at sea every day. The moment when the ideas presented during the year become a public heritage.

Workshops for schools

Educational paths on the link between the history of the tuna fishery, climate and pollution: from the tuna routes of the past to today's challenges, to train the sea guardians of tomorrow.

Network of tuna fisheries

The idea of building a network between the Mediterranean tuna fishery museums: sharing archives, experiences and common initiatives for the protection of the sea and its traditions.

Get involved too

Whether you are a school, an association, a scientific body or a citizen with an idea: the museum is open to proposals. Tell us about your initiative on the Get Involved page.
